  <h3 class="book-title text-[var(--book-title-1)] text-4xl"> 5.3 Aquifers and Aquitards</h3>

  <hr class="linea-delgada bg-[var(--rayas)]"></hr>

  This system consists of an upper clay unit (aquitard) separating an upper unconfined aquifer from a lower confined sand aquifer. <strong>Potentiometric contours</strong> connect points of equal hydraulic head and, in isotropic aquifers with predominantly horizontal flow, are approximately vertical lines. The <strong>potentiometric surface</strong> represents the level to which water would rise in a well open to the confined aquifer.

  The flow direction through the aquitard is determined by comparing the hydraulic head above and below the low-conductivity unit. In the case of <strong>Figure 27a</strong>, the water table is above the potentiometric surface, so flow through the clay is <strong>downward</strong> (recharge).

  <ul>

    <li><strong>The potentiometric surface</strong> is represented by a planar surface (a straight line in cross-section) connecting the head elevations of the two wells.</li>
    <li><strong>The direction of groundwater flow through the upper clay unit</strong> is mainly downward (recharge). This is determined by comparing the water table elevation to the potentiometric surface elevation. Since the water table is higher, there is a downward vertical gradient.</li>
    <li><strong>The expected vertical hydraulic head profile</strong> near Well 2 shows a relatively steep downward gradient through the clay, followed by a relatively constant head (or a much slighter gradient) through the highly conductive sand.</li>
